02Featured research

ARTIST: plug-and-play protein biosensors

Cells control genes with proteins that bind DNA. ARTIST (Aptamer-Regulated Transcription for In vitro Sensing and Transduction) borrows that logic: a DNA aptamer built into a transcription template acts as the binding site. When the target protein binds, transcription is repressed, so the RNA output reports how much protein is there. Recognition and signal processing are decoupled, so swapping the aptamer retargets the sensor without redesigning the circuit.

03Publications

Selected first-author work

Six first- and co-first-author papers across molecular biosensing, soft robotics and bioinspired adhesives. * equal contribution

Nature Communications2024

Plug-and-play protein biosensors using aptamer-regulated in vitro transcription

H. J. Lee, T. Xie, B. Kang, X. Yu, S. W. Schaffter, R. Schulman

A modular way to build protein biosensors: aptamers embedded in DNA transcription templates repress transcription when their target binds, turning protein concentration into an RNA signal. Swapping aptamers retargets the sensor, as shown for thrombin and the cytokines IL‑6, TNF‑α and IFN‑γ. Pairing it with different downstream circuits gives analog or digital readouts, with detection ranges tunable over two orders of magnitude.

Advanced Science2022

Soft microdenticles on artificial octopus sucker enable extraordinary adaptability and wet adhesion on diverse nonflat surfaces

G. W. Hwang*, H. J. Lee*, D. W. Kim, T.-H. Yang, C. Pang

Octopus suckers grip rough, curved rocks with the help of tiny tooth-like denticles. Recreating them as soft microdenticles on an artificial sucker yields a switchable adhesive that conforms to diverse non-flat surfaces. It reaches ≈29 N on dry and ≈49 N on wet flat substrates, and ≈17 N even on very rough surfaces.

ACS Nano2021

An electronically perceptive bioinspired soft wet-adhesion actuator with carbon nanotube-based strain sensors

H. J. Lee*, S. Baik*, G. W. Hwang*, J. H. Song, D. W. Kim, T.-H. Yang, C. Pang

A soft, octopus-inspired suction actuator that can feel what it holds. Carbon-nanotube strain sensors laid across the sucker’s uneven surface read out deformation patterns, so the gripper can adhere in wet conditions and recognize the objects it grabs.

Biosensors and Bioelectronics2020

Recent advances in three-dimensional microelectrode array technologies for in vitro and in vivo cardiac and neuronal interfaces

J. S. Choi*, H. J. Lee*, S. Rajaraman, D.-H. Kim

A review of three-dimensional microelectrode arrays for recording the electrophysiology of cardiac and neural cells, organized by geometry, materials and fabrication. It also covers their in vivo uses in heart and brain diagnostics and therapeutics.

Advanced Materials2019

Bioinspired adhesive architectures: from skin patch to integrated bioelectronics

S. Baik*, H. J. Lee*, D. W. Kim, J. W. Kim, Y. Lee, C. Pang

A review of how nature’s adhesive structures inform engineered ones: their architectures, attachment physics and fabrication. It follows the medical devices they enable, from skin patches to integrated multifunctional bioelectronics.

ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces2019

A capillarity-enhanced organ-attachable adhesive with highly drainable wrinkled octopus-inspired architectures

S. Baik*, H. J. Lee*, D. W. Kim, H. Min, C. Pang

Inspired by the soft wrinkles inside small octopus suckers, this patch drains interfacial liquid and uses capillarity to adhere to wet organs (tested on porcine heart and liver). A soft siloxane layer keeps it gripping dry and sweaty skin.
