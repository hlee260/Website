Biography

Programming nucleic acids to sense.

I’m a postdoctoral scholar at Stanford University, where I combine computational structure prediction with experiments to design RNA aptamers that bind small-molecule ligands.

I completed my Ph.D. in Biomedical Engineering at Johns Hopkins University with Rebecca Schulman, building molecular biosensors, DNA/RNA networks and reaction cascades that compute on biomolecules. My dissertation introduced ARTIST, a plug-and-play platform that turns aptamer–protein binding into programmable transcriptional outputs (Nature Communications, 2024).

Before that, I studied Chemical Engineering at Sungkyunkwan University (B.S., then M.S. with Changhyun Pang), engineering octopus-inspired adhesives for skin- and organ-attachable patches, bioelectronics and soft robotics.

Research highlight

ARTIST (Aptamer-Regulated Transcription for In vitro Sensing and Transduction) borrows a trick from gene regulation: a DNA aptamer built into a transcription template acts as the binding site. When the target protein binds, transcription is repressed, so the RNA output reports how much protein is there. Recognition and signal processing are decoupled, so swapping the aptamer retargets the sensor without redesigning the circuit. Try it:

  1. Plug-and-play protein biosensors using aptamer-regulated in vitro transcription

    H. J. Lee, T. Xie, B. Kang, X. Yu, S. W. Schaffter, R. Schulman

    Nature Communications 15, 7973 · 2024

    Aptamers built into transcription templates repress transcription when their target binds, so swapping the aptamer retargets the sensor (thrombin, IL‑6, TNF‑α, IFN‑γ), with analog or digital readouts tunable over two orders of magnitude.

    G. W. Hwang*, H. J. Lee*, D. W. Kim, T.-H. Yang, C. Pang

    Advanced Science 9, 2202978 · 2022

    Soft, tooth-like microdenticles modeled on octopus suckers make a switchable adhesive that conforms to curved and rough surfaces: ≈29 N dry, ≈49 N wet and ≈17 N on very rough substrates.

    Paper Free full text Issue cover Co-first

    H. J. Lee*, S. Baik*, G. W. Hwang*, J. H. Song, D. W. Kim, T.-H. Yang, C. Pang

    ACS Nano 15, 14137–14148 · 2021

    An octopus-inspired suction actuator that can feel what it holds: carbon-nanotube strain sensors across the sucker read out deformation, so the gripper adheres in wet conditions and recognizes objects.
